Listen to radio without burning your data

Updated 2026-10-30

Internet radio is cheaper on data than almost anything else you stream — a long way below video, and well below most music services at their default quality. But it runs for hours rather than minutes, and that is where the bill comes from. Here is what it actually costs, and nine things that reduce it.

What an hour of radio really costs

The pattern is simple: halving the bitrate halves the data. Moving from a 128 kbps stream to a 64 kbps AAC stream cuts your monthly use in half, and on a phone speaker or in a car most people cannot tell the two apart.

1. Pick the low-bitrate version of the same station

Large stations publish several streams of the same programme — often 128 kbps for desktop and 48 or 64 kbps for mobile. They carry identical content. If a directory lists a station more than once, the lower number is the one to keep.

2. Prefer AAC over MP3

At the same bitrate, AAC sounds clearly better than MP3. A 64 kbps AAC stream is roughly comparable to a 128 kbps MP3 one, for half the data. When a station offers both, there is no reason to choose MP3 on mobile.

3. Talk and news do not need music quality

The human voice occupies a narrow frequency range. A news or talk station at 32 to 48 kbps is perfectly clear, and uses roughly a third of what a music stream does. Reserve the higher bitrates for the stations you listen to with headphones on.

4. Close the tab, do not just lock the screen

A stream keeps downloading as long as it is playing, screen on or off. Locking the phone changes nothing. Press stop when you stop listening — the most common cause of a surprising data bill is a station that ran all night in a forgotten tab.

5. Use a sleep timer at night

If you listen while falling asleep, a sleep timer is the single most effective data-saving setting there is. Eight hours of unattended streaming at 128 kbps costs around 460 MB; a 30-minute timer costs 29 MB.

6. Set a data warning in your phone settings

Both Android and iOS can show per-app data use and warn you at a threshold you choose. Set the warning a little below your monthly allowance rather than at it, so you have room to adjust before the cap arrives.

7. Favourite your stations instead of browsing

Every station you sample loads a few seconds of audio before you decide. Ten stations sampled is a small but real cost, repeated daily. Building a favourites list once means you go straight to what you want afterwards.

8. Use Wi-Fi for the long sessions

This is obvious but worth stating plainly: the hours you spend at home or at work are the bulk of your listening. Moving only those onto Wi-Fi usually removes most of the problem without changing anything else.

9. Watch out for stations that reconnect constantly

An unstable station that drops and reconnects every minute re-buffers each time, downloading the same few seconds repeatedly. It also sounds bad. If a station does this consistently, replace it — a reliable station at a higher bitrate often costs less in practice than an unreliable one at a low bitrate.

The short version

  • 64 kbps AAC for music on mobile, 48 kbps for talk.
  • Stop the stream instead of locking the screen.
  • Sleep timer every night.
  • Long sessions on Wi-Fi.

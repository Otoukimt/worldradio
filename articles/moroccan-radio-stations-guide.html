Moroccan radio stations: a complete listening guide

Updated October 2026 · 11 min read

Moroccan radio is one of the most listened-to media in the country.

How Moroccan radio got here

For most of the twentieth century, broadcasting in Morocco meant the state.

The High Authority for Audiovisual Communication was created in 2002.

In May 2006, HACA issued its first wave of private radio licences.

Two stations sit outside that story.

The public stations: SNRT

The SNRT runs several national radio channels alongside regional stations.

Al Idaa Al Watania

The flagship Arabic channel, broadcast from Rabat.

Chaîne Inter

The multilingual channel, built around French.

Radio Amazighe

Broadcasting in Tamazight, Tachelhit and Tarifit.

Radio Mohammed VI of the Holy Quran

Continuous Quran recitation and religious programming.

The private stations, by what they actually do

Medi1 Radio

Founded in 1980 and broadcast from Tangier.

Hit Radio

Launched in 2008 from Rabat, aimed at listeners under thirty.

Chada FM

A generalist music and talk station with a strong Casablanca base.

Aswat

Generalist, with more weight given to economy and society.

Radio Mars

Sport, and specifically football.

MFM, Radio Plus, Cap Radio and the regional networks

MFM operates as a proximity network for the Atlas, Saïss and Souss regions.

Atlantic Radio and Luxe Radio

Both lean towards business and economics.

Listening from outside Morocco

Nearly every station above streams online, and none are geo-blocked.

  • Some stations still publish insecure http links.
  • Morocco is on UTC+1.
  • Ramadan schedules change completely.

A note on language

Moroccan radio moves between Darija, Arabic, French and Amazigh.

Why Moroccan radio is worth your time

Streaming platforms have flattened music discovery into a global sameness.

Radio in The United States Of America

American radio began as a hobby in a garage and became the most commercial broadcasting system on earth. It never had a national public broadcaster funded by a licence fee; it had a market, and the market invented almost every radio format the world now uses. That is why so many people search for US radio live and want to listen to American radio online.

A garage in Pittsburgh

In 1920 a Westinghouse engineer, Frank Conrad, was playing records over a transmitter for fellow amateurs in Pittsburgh. Enough people bought receivers to hear him that his employer saw a business, and KDKA went on air in November 1920 with presidential election results. Within a few years hundreds of stations were competing for the same frequencies.

The network age

The Radio Act of 1927 and the Communications Act of 1934 brought order to the airwaves, but kept the commercial principle. NBC launched in 1926 and CBS a year later, and by the 1930s a single programme could reach the whole country. It was the era of Roosevelt's fireside chats and of Orson Welles's The War of the Worlds in 1938, which showed how powerful a voice on the radio had become.

Saved by formats and FM

Television took the stars and the drama in the 1950s. Radio survived by narrowing its focus: Top 40, country, all-news, talk, R&B and Spanish-language stations each aimed at a precise audience. FM, ignored for years, turned out to be ideal for music and overtook AM by the 1980s.

Public radio from the side door

National Public Radio was created in 1970 and went on air in 1971. Funded by listeners and sponsors, it and its member stations carry the long-form reporting that commercial formats rarely make room for.

Still the biggest reach

The Telecommunications Act of 1996 allowed large companies to own many more stations, and consolidation followed quickly. Yet radio still reaches more American adults each month than any other medium, and news and talk lead the ratings, especially in the car.
